How a Consultation Works

Everything starts from your kundli (birth chart) — built from your date, time, and place of birth. The chart maps where each planet sat at the moment you were born, and Vedic astrology reads the relationships between those planetary positions to explain patterns in health, career, marriage, and finances.

Two traditions inform the practice:

  • Vedic astrology — the classical Indian system, based on the sidereal zodiac (adjusted for the Earth's slow wobble), which is why Vedic predictions often differ from Western sun-sign astrology for the same birth date.
  • Lal Kitab — a separate remedial tradition, known for simple, often household-based remedies (rather than elaborate rituals) for planetary afflictions.

What Gets Diagnosed, and What It's Used For

Kundli Matching for Marriage

Before marriage, both partners' birth charts are compared — known as kundli milan — to check compatibility across health, temperament, and prosperity. This includes checking for Manglik dosh (Mars affliction), one of the most commonly flagged factors in Indian matchmaking, and whether it needs to be offset by a matching dosh on the other side or a specific remedy.

Career and Business Guidance

Your chart is read for which planetary periods (dashas) are currently active, and whether the placement supports or works against the career direction you're pursuing — useful when a decision (job change, new venture, expansion) needs timing.

Graha Dosh and Planetary Remedies

When a planet is poorly placed or afflicted in the chart, it's read as a graha dosh — often linked to recurring problems in one area of life. Remedies vary by tradition and by which planet is involved: gemstones, specific rituals, or Lal Kitab-style household remedies.

Horoscope and Birth Chart Reading

A full read of your chart — planetary placements, current and upcoming dasha periods, and what they typically indicate — for anyone who wants the bigger picture rather than a single problem-focused reading.

Vedic Astrology vs Western Astrology

The two systems don't just differ in style — they use different zodiacs. Western astrology uses the tropical zodiac, fixed to the seasons. Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac, adjusted for the actual position of constellations today, which have shifted from where they were when the tropical system was set. This is why your Vedic sun sign can come out differently from your Western one, and why the two traditions can give different-sounding readings for the same birth details.
